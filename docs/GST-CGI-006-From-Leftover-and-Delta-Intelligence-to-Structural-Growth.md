## Abstract

A structural-intelligence runtime should not force every input into an existing mature branch.

GST-CGI therefore treats **Leftover** as a first-class runtime outcome.

Leftover means that the current mature structure, evidence, or policy context is insufficient to resolve a case reliably. It is not merely an exception, error, or miscellaneous bucket.

Once Leftover becomes explicit, however, another obligation appears:

> **What should the system do with unresolved structural experience?**

This article develops the connection between **Leftover**, **Delta Intelligence**, and **Structural Growth**.

The central growth loop is:

```text id="plrrpu"
LEFTOVER
    ↓
Evidence Accumulation
    ↓
Structural Comparison
    ↓
Delta Discovery
    ↓
Candidate Difference
    ↓
A/B Validation
    ↓
Candidate Two-Way CCC
    ↓
Policy-Governed Promotion
    ↓
New Mature Structural Memory
```

The key proposition is:

> **Once Leftover becomes a first-class runtime outcome, Delta Intelligence becomes a first-class runtime obligation.**

The system should not merely remember that it failed to resolve a case. It should preserve enough evidence, provenance, context, counter-evidence, and structural information to determine whether repeated unresolved cases reveal a stable and decision-relevant new difference.

That difference may eventually become a new Generalized Structural Trigger, comparator, Two-Way CCC, local branch, evidence contract, policy perspective, or other computational structure.

This is the transition from merely accumulating experience to **growing the computational structure through which future experience is interpreted**.

A stronger structural-intelligence runtime asks:

> Why was this case unresolved?

> Does this unresolved case belong to a recurring structural difference that the current system has not yet represented?

This transforms Leftover from:


```text id="wqcw7n"
Structural Growth Input
```



> **Structural growth should search not only for evidence that creates a branch, but also for evidence that prevents a bad branch from being created.**

This connects Delta Intelligence with Counter-Evidence Intelligence.

# 53. Canonical Structural-Growth Principles

## Principle 1 — Leftover Is First-Class

> **A governed structural runtime should preserve unresolved cases explicitly rather than forcing them into mature branches.**

---

## Principle 2 — Leftover Is a Growth Input

> **Leftover is not merely an error state; it is a primary source of evidence for future structural discovery.**

---

## Principle 3 — Delta Intelligence Is Obligatory

> **Once Leftover becomes a first-class runtime outcome, Delta Intelligence becomes a first-class runtime obligation.**

---

## Principle 4 — More Experience Is Not More Structure

> **Experience accumulation and structural discovery are different processes.**

---

## Principle 5 — Candidate Difference Is Not Mature Structure

> **A discovered difference must be validated before it becomes a trusted computational structure.**

---

## Principle 6 — Two-Way CCC Is a Minimal Growth Unit

> **A validated local A/B difference provides a simple, composable unit for incremental structural growth.**

---

## Principle 7 — Counter-Evidence Governs Growth Quality

> **Structural promotion should search for evidence against a candidate difference, not only evidence supporting it.**

---

## Principle 8 — Promotion Is Governed

> **Structural intelligence may discover new structure; the Policy Control Plane determines whether and where that structure receives runtime authority.**

---

## Principle 9 — Structural Growth Is Broader Than Branch Growth

> **Triggers, metrics, comparators, evidence contracts, behaviors, policy perspectives, CCCs, and delegation interfaces may all become objects of structural growth.**

---

## Principle 10 — Growth Changes Future Computation

> **Structural growth changes the topology, capability, cost, and potentially the explainability of future computation.**
